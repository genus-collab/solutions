### F1 (minor). Two different meanings of `\cn` in the unit
- Where: s03_gauge_5.tex l.20 and l.31 (lem:gau-diag) versus s03_gauge_6.tex l.25 (def:gau-can), l.30 and l.43 (prop:gau-symp).
- In lem:gau-diag, c(0) is "as in thm:gau-U (b)", i.e. P = P♮ c with P♮_0 = d Θ_U. In prop:gau-symp, c(z) := P_11(0,z), and in def:gau-can / thm:gau-G (b) c is defined by P = Pc c. These are different series: P_11(0,z) = c_U(z) P♮_11(0,z), and already at z = 0, P_11(0,0) = c_U(0) d^U_1(0) = c_U(0) (b̃_0/b_0)(0). They agree at z = 0 only under (N0) and to all orders only if P♮ is normalised like Pc.
- No statement is affected: lem:gau-diag uses only c_U(0) and its proof passes correctly from p_{0,1}(0) = 1 to c_U(0) = 1 through d^U_1(0) = 1 under (N0); prop:gau-symp uses only its own definition. A reader combining the general (non-(N0)) part of lem:gau-diag with prop:gau-symp could be misled. Repair: write c_U(0) or `(P_0)_{00}(0)` in lem:gau-diag (lem:gau-lead identifies it as (P_0)_{00}(0)).

### F2 (minor). lem:gau-HPF: labels and one omitted (trivial) case

### F3 (minor). lem:gau-vanq (a): the pulled-back model for a general A
- Where: s03_gauge_5.tex l.81 and l.89.
- The lemma is stated for an arbitrary one-pole system A over Q[[u]], but its second sentence refers to "the pulled-back model" without saying along which v. The proof needs Ã to have the one-pole shape (to apply (c) and lem:gau-uniq to Ã), i.e. v ∈ uQ[[u]] with v'(0) ≠ 0; this is automatic under the stated hypothesis b_0(0) = b̃_0(0) = v'(0) ≠ 0 once v ∈ uQ[[u]] is assumed.
