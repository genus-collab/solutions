## 2. What the source says (v3)

### 2.1 Section 1.1 (standing assumptions; the recursion (1.1))
Input: a compact Riemann surface S with a chosen basis of A- and B-cycles; two meromorphic functions x, y on S such that all
critical points p_1..p_a of x are simple, y is regular at p_i and dy|_{p_i} != 0; a Bergman kernel B (double pole on the diagonal
with biresidue 1, holomorphic elsewhere, vanishing A-periods). Output: omega^{(0)}_{1,0} := -y dx, omega^{(0)}_{2,0} := B and, for 2g-2+m > 0,

(1.1)  omega^{(g)}_{m,0}(z_[m]) = 1/2 sum_i res_{z=p_i} [ int_z^{sigma_i(z)} omega^{(0)}_{2,0}(z_1,.) / (omega^{(0)}_{1,0}(sigma_i z) - omega^{(0)}_{1,0}(z)) ]
        ( omega^{(g-1)}_{m+1,0}(z, sigma_i z, z_rest) + sum'_{(g_i,|I_i|) != (0,0)} omega^{(g_1)}(z, z_{I_1}) omega^{(g_2)}(sigma_i z, z_{I_2}) ).

Remark 1.1: the requirements could be relaxed, but the paper works with globally meromorphic x, y.
Section 5.1 of v3 (source l.1948): "Let p_1,...,p_a in S be the zeros of dx. We assume that all zeros of dx are simple." So "critical
points" means zeros of dx, not ramification points of x : S -> P^1 at poles; the examples (7.1)-(7.3), which have poles of x of order
> 1, confirm this reading.

### 2.2 Theorem 1.14 (the base of Theorem 7.1; Theorem 1.8 is the conjecture form, Theorem 1.13 the equivalence of (1.11) and (1.13); all three proved in v3 Sec. 5.3)
"Assume the differentials omega^{(g)}_{m,0} satisfy TR for (S,x,y,B) with meromorphic x and y, all zeros of dy are simple, and x is
regular at the zeros of dy. Then the omega^{(g)}_{0,n} given by (1.13) satisfy TR for (S,y,x,B)." (Unstable terms (1.14):
omega^{(0)}_{0,1} = -x dy, omega^{(0)}_{0,2} = B.) The proof (Sec. 5.3) also uses "the zero loci of dx and dy are disjoint" (implied by
dy|_{p_i} != 0 in Sec. 1.1) and Prop. 5.8 (zeros of dx and dy simple and pairwise distinct; omega_{m,0} without poles at
the zeros of dy). Item (4) of the pole analysis treats poles of y (local form y = z^{-s}, x = z; "x might also be nonzero at that point or
even have a pole of some degree, but it will only improve the cancellation").

### 2.3 Section 7.1 and Theorem 7.1 (v3 numbering confirmed on the v3 PDF)
Preamble: "for all possible rational x such that dx has only simple zeros, we give an explicit closed formula". For y(z) = z, dy = dz has
no zeros, so on the swapped side all stable omega^{(g)}_{0,n} vanish, and (7.5) omega^{(0)}_{0,1} = -x(z) dz, omega^{(0)}_{0,2} = B.
"Using Equation (1.13) in the opposite direction ... we obtain":

- (7.6) W_i = -exp(-w_i (S(w_i hbar d_{z_i}) - 1) x(z_i)) dz_i / w_i;

### 3.1 Kernel (1.1) versus K_b (conv:mod-kernel)
With omega^{(0)}_{1,0} = -y dx and x(sigma z) = x(z) (so sigma^* dx = dx):
omega_{1,0}(sigma z) - omega_{1,0}(z) = (y(z) - y(sigma z)) dx(z), and int_z^{sigma z} B = -int_{sigma z}^{z} B.
Hence the ABDKS kernel is -(1/2) int_{sigma z}^{z} B(z_1,.)/((y(z) - y(sigma z)) dx(z)) = -K_b(z_1, z), with q = z, iota_b = sigma_i. The
bracket of (1.1) (exclusion (g_i,|I_i|) != (0,0)) is the bracket of eq:mod-kernel (the two omega_{0,1} terms omitted); omega_{0,2} terms are
kept in both. Induction: if omega_{h',n'} = (-1)^{n'} omega^A_{h',n'} for all lower terms (true for (0,2), both being B, and also for (0,1):
y dx = -(-y dx)), the bracket for omega_{h,n+1} is (-1)^n times the ABDKS bracket (|I_1| + |I_2| = n, and n + 2 has the parity of n), and the
kernel contributes another factor -1, so omega_{h,n+1} = (-1)^{n+1} omega^A_{h,n+1}. The dictionary of s06_model.tex l.20-21 and
s06_model_7.tex l.20 holds. (The EO half of the sentence in conv:mod-kernel is outside this unit.)

### 3.2 Hypotheses for the model curve
Theorem 7.1 is Theorem 1.14 applied to the swapped data (S, x', y') = (P^1, z, x(z)), with conclusion for (S, y', x') = (P^1, x(z), z).

- S = P^1 compact; B = dz1 dz2/(z1-z2)^2 is the unique Bergman kernel: yes.
- x' = p, y' = W_v meromorphic: yes.
- TR on (P^1, p, W_v) is defined and trivial: dp has no zeros on P^1 (it has a double pole at infinity), so Sec. 1.1 is vacuous and the stable
  outputs are 0 (Prop. 5.3 of v3: no poles allowed; holomorphic differentials on P^1 vanish).
- "All zeros of dy' = dW_v are simple": dW_v = (p^5 - v) p^{-2} dp has the five simple zeros p^5 = v (v != 0), and poles of order 2 at 0 and
  5 at infinity (5 - 7 = -2, as it must be).
- "x' = p regular at the zeros of dy'": the zeros are finite points. (This is where a rational x with a critical point at z = infinity would be
  excluded; that is not the case here.)
- Prop. 5.8: the zeros of dx' (none) and of dy' are simple and pairwise distinct; omega'_{m,0} (stable ones 0,
  omega'^{(0)}_{1,0} = -W_v dp, B) have no poles at p^5 = v: yes.
- The target TR (P^1, W_v, p) satisfies Sec. 1.1: the critical points of W_v (zeros of dW_v) are simple; y = p is regular there; dy = dp != 0.
  The order-4 pole of W_v at infinity is a ramification point of W_v : P^1 -> P^1 but not a zero of dW_v, hence not a "critical point" in the
  sense of ABDKS (their Sec. 5.1, and their hypermap example (7.2), which is this curve for r = 5 up to rescaling).
- Item (4) of the proof of Thm 1.14 (poles of y' = W_v): at p = 0, y' has a simple pole and x' = p a simple zero (the case written out); at
  p = infinity, y' has a pole of order 4 and x' a simple pole (the case "x has a pole", dismissed in one line by ABDKS). Independent degree count
  for this curve: in a term of (7.9), the coefficient of dz_i has degree in z_i at most 4 - 10 sum_l k_l - sum_{e at i}(2k_e + 2 + 4a_e), where the
  k_l are the hbar-orders of the vertex exponent factors and a_e >= 1 is the w_i-degree contributed by the edge e (with its hbar^{2k_e}); this is
  <= -2 as soon as the vertex has an edge or positive hbar-order, so every term of (7.9) with 2g-2+m > 0 is holomorphic at z_i = infinity.
  (For m = 1 this is Step 2 of prop:mod-period.) ABDKS's one-line remark is therefore borne out here.

Conclusion: Theorem 7.1 applies to x = W_v, y = p, v != 0.

### 3.3 Symbols
- D_{x_i} eta = d(eta/dx_i): as in Thm 7.1. [hbar^{2g}], [w_i^{r_i}]: coefficient extractions applied to everything to their right (list after (1.11)).
- g(Gamma) = first Betti number (list after (1.11)); Gamma connected simple graphs on the vertices 1..m; products over E(Gamma) and V(Gamma) = {1..m}.
- Derivation check ((1.13) in the opposite direction): the vertex factor is (-1)(dz_i/dx_i)(1/w_i) exp(w_i S(hbar w_i d_z)(-x) - w_i(-x)), which
  gives W_i after multiplication by dx_i (the subtraction of the omega^{(0)}_{0,1} term produces S - 1); multiedges carry only omega^{(0)}_{0,2} = B
  (the stable omega_{0,n} vanish); loops vanish, since the regularised B - dy dy/(y-y)^2 is 0 for y = z; k parallel edges between i and j, with
  weight 1/k! and Betti contribution hbar^{2(k-1)}, sum to (e^{hbar^2 A} - 1)/hbar^2 = W_{i,j}. This is the origin of the factor 1/hbar^2.
- Closed form of (7.7): hbar^2 w_i w_j S S (z_i-z_j)^{-2} = Delta_i Delta_j log(z_i - z_j), Delta f = f(z + w hbar/2) - f(z - w hbar/2), whose
  exponential is (d^2 - (a-b)^2)/(d^2 - (a+b)^2), d = z_i - z_j, a = w_i hbar/2, b = w_j hbar/2; minus 1, divided by hbar^2, this is
  w_i w_j/(d^2 - hbar^2 (w_i+w_j)^2/4). Its geometric expansion is the manuscript's sum_k (hbar^2/4)^k (w_i+w_j)^{2k} w_i w_j (z_i-z_j)^{-2k-2}.
  Checked by wolframscript to order hbar^6 (work/L4/chk/wij.wls, difference 0).
- Range: (7.9) at (g,m) = (0,2) gives B; at (0,1) it gives 0 (W_1 at hbar^0 is -dz/w, whose [w^0] is 0), not -y dx: the term
  delta_{(g,n),(0,1)}(-x_1) of (1.13) is dropped in Thm 7.1, whose "all differentials" is literally true only for (g,m) != (0,1). (7.10) starts
  at g = 1, correctly.
- Signs: (7.10) for the Airy curve x = z^2/2, y = z gives omega^{(1)}_{1,0} = +dz/(8z^4) (W_1 = -e^{-w^3 hbar^2/24} dz/w; [hbar^2][w^2] = 1/24;
  D_x^2 dz = 3 dz/z^4); direct TR with K_b gives omega_{1,1} = Res_{q=0} -dp_0 dq/(8q^3(p_0^2 - q^2)) = -dp_0/(8p_0^4). Consistent with
  omega_{1,1} = -omega^A_{1,1} and with ABDKS's own recovery of <tau_{3g-2}>_g = 1/(24^g g!).

## 5. Findings

### F1 (minor) The published numbering differs from the cited v3 numbering
Location: s01_intro_3.tex l.9; s06_model_7.tex l.10, l.15; refs.bib l.93-97 (ABDKS25).
The manuscript cites (7.6), (7.7), (7.9), (7.10) of v3, and the bibliography says so. In the JEMS version (the DOI of the entry), W_i is
(7.4), W_{i,j} is unnumbered, the closed formula is (7.5), the one-point formula is unnumbered (and the base formula (1.13) is (1.7)).
Theorem 7.1 and (1.1) are unchanged. Not an error under the declared convention, but a reader following the DOI will not find (7.6)-(7.10)
as cited.
Repair: add the JEMS numbers, e.g. "(7.6) = JEMS (7.4), (7.9) = JEMS (7.5); (7.7) and (7.10) are unnumbered displays there".

### F2 (minor) Hypotheses of Theorem 7.1 paraphrased incompletely
Location: s06_model_7.tex l.5 and l.20 (sec:mod-Lreg, before lem:mod-abdks).
l.20 lists the Sec. 1.1 assumptions as "(simple critical points of x, dy != 0 there)"; Sec. 1.1 also requires y regular at the critical
points, S compact and B normalised, and Thm 7.1 is derived from Thm 1.14 applied to the swapped data (P^1, z, x(z)), whose hypotheses are
"zeros of dx simple, z regular at them". Theorem 7.1 as printed says "Let x(z) be an arbitrary rational function"; the simple-zero condition
appears only in the preamble of Sec. 7. The manuscript's added hypothesis ("dx has only simple zeros") is the correct reading, and all the
conditions hold for x = W_v (zeros p^5 = v finite and simple, p regular there; the order-4 pole at infinity is not a critical point in ABDKS's
sense). No effect on validity.
Repair: state "S = P^1; the zeros of dx are simple and finite (so y = p is regular there) and dy = dp != 0; these are the hypotheses of
[ABDKS, Sec. 1.1] and of [ABDKS, Thm 1.14] for the swapped data (P^1, p, W_v), whose recursion is trivial since dp has no zeros".

### F3 (minor) Range of (7.9) not stated; (0,1) excluded
Location: s06_model_7.tex l.5-9 (eq:mod-abdks).
"the differentials ... are" (7.9), without a range, as in ABDKS ("all differentials"); for (g,m) = (0,1) the right side of (7.9) is 0, not
-y dx (Thm 7.1 drops the delta_{(g,n),(0,1)} term of (1.13)). The manuscript uses (7.9) only for 2h-2+n > 0 (lem:mod-abdks) and (7.10) only
for h >= 1, so nothing is affected.
Repair: add "for 2g-2+m > 0 (and (g,m) = (0,2))".

No other mismatch was found: the signs, the factor 1/hbar^2, S(u), D_x, the graph sum and its Betti exponent, the role of omega_{0,1} (only
through the kernel on the model side; through the S - 1 of the exponent on the swapped side) and of omega_{0,2} (= B; loops regularised away)
are as the manuscript uses them. The manuscript never uses the swapped curve (p, W_v) itself, nor any symplectic invariance of free energies
under the swap (which would fail here); the free energies are computed from omega_{h,1} directly.
