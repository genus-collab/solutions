# L6 -- Literature audit: the genus-0 mirror theorem (M) and the remaining quoted formulas

Status: COMPLETE. (Report written by referee unit L6; sources in work/L6/src, small scripts in work/L6/scripts.)

Verdict in one paragraph. Every quoted statement of this unit says, in its source, what the manuscript takes
from it, in compatible conventions, and its hypotheses hold where it is applied. In particular the "pin" of (M)
after thm:set-mirror (s02_setup.tex l.85-93) transcribes Givental's Theorem 11.6 (arXiv v2) exactly
(Q-substitution, the factor E_i, the factor f, the definitions of f, g_l, Z_i, Z_i^*), the elimination of
lambda_i from the exponent is correct, the misprint of Cor. 11.7 is real, and the translation (a)-(d) to
J(TH,z) = I/C_0 is correct step by step, including the lambda-dependent factor C_0 exp(e_1(lambda) g_1/(z C_0))
before the specialisation (independently confirmed by the z^0 term of the equivariant I-function). The
J-function normalisation agrees with Coates-Givental (10). The definitions of N_{g,d}, F_g agree with
Lho-Pandharipande (both papers). Arbarello-Cornalba (1.7) and kappa_0 = 2g-2+n are stated as used, and the
dilaton lemma uses them within their range. DLMF 24.2.3 and 24.4.1 are quoted with the right signs and
conventions; lem:fsp-dlmf5118 coincides term by term with DLMF 5.11.8 (checked symbolically to order 9).
No fatal, gap or fixable finding. Six minor findings: (m1) Zagier-Zinger arXiv:0710.0889 does not contain the
specialisation lambda_i = zeta^i (only the resulting hypergeometric series); (m2) [GJR18, Section 3.1, (9)]:
equation (9) is in Section 3.2 (the specialisation of Section 3.1 is unnumbered); (m3) "J at Q=1, t = T(q)H"
with T containing the formal log is meaningful only via the divisor equation and the convention e^{log q} = q,
which is used but not stated; (m4) the parenthetical justification in def:set-fq that the localisation rational
function can be specialised at lambda_i = zeta^i treats only the R^1 term; (m5) b_signs.tex l.34 says only the
B_{2k} enter, but Bernoulli polynomials B_{5l+1}(1/5) enter thm:gam-ginf, where the convention matters (the
note's convention is DLMF's, so nothing is wrong); (m6) bookkeeping: the DLMF bib annote cites 5.11.1, 5.11.8,
2.3.8 that the text no longer cites, and 24.4.1 is derived in-line, so Theorem Gamma (b) needs no quoted input.

### 1.1 (M): Givental, Theorem 11.6 (n=4, r=1, l_1=5, lambda'=0), set-up of Section 9, Prop. 11.4 -- VERIFIED

What the source says (arXiv v2):
- Section 9 (p.23): E_d = Euler class of the bundle over Y_{2,d} (genus-0, degree d, two markings x_0, x_1) with
  fibre H^0(C, phi^*H^{l_1} + ...); S_d(hbar) = E_d/(hbar + c_1^{(0)}), c_1^{(0)} = first Chern class of the
  universal tangent line at x_0 (so c_1^{(0)} = -psi_0).
- p.26: T acts on C^{n+1}; H_T^*(CP^n) = C[p,lambda]/((p-lambda_0)...(p-lambda_n)); "-p" is c_1 of the Hopf
  bundle; phi_i := prod_{j != i}(p - lambda_j); the fibrewise torus T' acts on (+)H^{l_j} with Euler class
  prod(l_j p - lambda'_j).
- p.27 (r = 1): E'_d = equivariant Euler class of the bundle of sections of psi^*(H^l) vanishing at x_0;
  Z_i := sum_{d>=0} q^d int_{Y_{2,d}} e_0^*(phi_i) (hbar+c)^{-1} E'_d; Prop. 9.6 gives Z_i = 1 + sum_{d>0}(...),
  i.e. the d = 0 term is 1.
- p.37: Section 11 works "in the Calabi-Yau case l_1 + ... + l_r = n + 1 (see Section 9 for a definition of
  Z_i)"; Prop. 11.4 (1)-(5): recursion, class P, uniqueness from the first two coefficients, invariance under
  (a) Z_i -> f(q) Z_i, (b) Z_i -> e^{lambda_i f(q)/hbar} Z_i(q e^{f(q)}), (c) Z_i -> exp(C f(q)/hbar) Z_i.
- p.39: Z_i^* = sum_d q^d prod_a prod_{m=1}^{l_a d}(l_a lambda_i - lambda'_a + m hbar) /
  prod_{alpha=0}^n prod_{m=1}^d (lambda_i - lambda_alpha + m hbar), with l_1+...+l_r = n+1.
- p.40: f = sum_d (l_1 d)!...(l_r d)!/(d!)^{n+1} q^d, g_l = sum_{d>=1} q^d prod_a (l_a d)!/(d!)^{n+1}
  (sum_{m=1}^{ld} 1/m); Prop. 11.5: Z_i^{(0)} = 1, Z_i^{(1)} = 0; Theorem 11.6: 1) put
  Q = q exp{sum_a l_a [g_{l_a}(q) - g_1(q)]/f(q)}; 2) multiply Z_i(Q(q),hbar) by
  exp{(1/(f hbar)) [sum_a (l_a lambda_i - lambda'_a) g_{l_a} - (sum_alpha (lambda_i - lambda_alpha)) g_1]};
  3) multiply all Z_i by f(q); "Then the resulting functions coincide with hypergeometric series Z_i^*(q,hbar)."
  Proof: by (b), (c), (a) and Prop. 11.4.
- p.41: s_i = e^{lambda_i T/hbar} Z_i(e^T, hbar); Corollary 11.7 multiplies by
  f(e^t) exp{[g_1(e^t)(sum_alpha l_alpha) - sum_a lambda'_a g_{l_a}(e^t)]/(hbar f(e^t))}.

Comparison with the manuscript (s02_setup.tex l.85-93, "The pin"):
- The definitions of Z_i, E'_d, c, f, g_l, Z_i^* and the three operations (1)-(3), with
  E_i = exp{(f hbar)^{-1}[sum_a(l_a lambda_i - lambda'_a)g_{l_a} - (sum_alpha(lambda_i - lambda_alpha))g_1]},
  are transcribed exactly. "Set-up in Section 9, pp. 23-27", "for r = 1 (p. 27)", "the proof rests on
  Proposition 11.4" are accurate page/statement references for v2.
- Algebra of the pin: Z_i^*(q) = f E_i Z_i(Q(q)). With s_i^*(t) = e^{lambda_i t/hbar} Z_i^*(e^t),
  s_i(T) = e^{lambda_i T/hbar} Z_i(e^T), e^T = Q(e^t): s_i^* = f E_i e^{-lambda_i(T-t)/hbar} s_i. The exponent is
  (f hbar)^{-1}[sum_a(l_a lambda_i - lambda'_a)g_{l_a} - ((n+1)lambda_i - e_1)g_1 - lambda_i sum_a l_a(g_{l_a}-g_1)]
  = (f hbar)^{-1}[e_1 g_1 - sum_a lambda'_a g_{l_a}] using sum_a l_a = n+1. Correct, and it is the formula of
  Cor. 11.7 with lambda_alpha in place of the printed l_alpha. The printed form is also inhomogeneous for the
  grading deg lambda = deg hbar = 1 of p.36, which confirms it is a misprint. The note's remark is right.
- Translation (a): f = C_0; g_5 - g_1 = sum q^d (5d)!/(d!)^5 sum_{m=d+1}^{5d} 1/m, so 5(g_5 - g_1) = I~_1 and
  Q = q exp(I~_1/C_0) = e^T (def:set-genus0). Matches also the mirror map printed in LP20 p.3.
- (b): at lambda' = 0 the factor is C_0 exp{e_1(lambda) g_1/(z C_0)}; e_1(zeta^0..zeta^4) = 0. Independent
  confirmation of sign and coefficient: the z^0-coefficient of the lambda-equivariant I-function is
  H(C_0 log q + 5(g_5 - g_1)) + e_1(lambda) g_1 (first-order expansion of the logs of the products), so
  I/C_0 = z + TH + e_1 g_1/C_0 + O(1/z); multiplying by exp(-e_1 g_1/(z C_0)) restores J = z + t + O(1/z).
- (c): with hbar = z, e^t = q, t = log q, lambda_i = zeta^i, lambda' = 0, s_i^* = I_i/z (the alpha = i factor
  in the denominator of Z_i^* is the j = i factor prod k z of I_i).
- (d): checked each step. eta-dual: x = ev_*(beta)/e_T(O(5)) since eta(x,gamma) = int x gamma e(O(5)).
  0 -> E' -> E_{0,1,d} -> ev^*O(5) -> 0 is exact with E' a bundle (R^1 of f^*O(5)(-x) vanishes on a genus-0
  tree of nonnegative degrees lowered by one at one point), so e(E_{0,1,d}) = e(E'_d) ev^*(5H). Divisor equation
  for descendants gives J(TH,z) = e^{TH/z}(z + sum_{d>=1} e^{dT} ev_*(E'_d/(z-psi))) (the d=0 part sums to
  z e^{TH/z}). On Givental's side, E'_d on Y_{2,d} is pulled back from M_{0,1}(P^4,d), pi_*(hbar-psi_0)^{-1} =
  hbar^{-1}(hbar-psi_0)^{-1} (pi_* psi_0^k = psi_0^{k-1}, pi_* 1 = 0), c = -psi_0, and
  int_{P^4} phi_i gamma = gamma|_{p_i}. Hence Z_i(q,z) = 1 + z^{-1} sum_{d>=1} q^d ev_*(E'_d/(z-psi))|_{p_i}
  and s_i(T) = J(TH,z)|_{p_i}/z. So C_0 J(TH,z)|_{p_i} = I_i for every i, which is (M).
- Hypotheses: Calabi-Yau (5 = n+1) holds. lambda' = 0 is a legitimate specialisation: the coefficients of
  Z_i (expanded in 1/z) are equivariant integrals of classes polynomial in lambda, lambda'; those of Z_i^* are
  rational in z with denominators products of (k z + linear form in lambda), whose 1/z-expansions are
  polynomial in lambda; E_i is exp of (polynomial in lambda, lambda') x (q-series)/z. The identity of
  Theorem 11.6 (rational functions in hbar coefficientwise in q, times E_i) maps injectively to an identity of
  1/z-expansions, and lambda -> zeta is a ring homomorphism there. The fibre weight of O(5) at p_i at
  lambda' = 0 is l lambda_i = 5 lambda_i, as in the note (and LP20 (10)). Givental's T-convention
  (p|_{p_i} = lambda_i, tangent weights lambda_i - lambda_j) is the note's.
- Use within hypotheses: (M) is used only in lem:set-birkhoff (a1_cyclicLR.tex l.64: J_0 = J/z = I/(zC_0) along
  the line, at lambda = zeta, Q = 1) and, through prop:set-cyclicLR and cor:set-cyclicPF5, in thm:gau-R0 and
  thm:con-glue. All uses are at lambda_i = zeta^i on the line t = T(q)H, exactly the form proved.
- Remark (not a finding): the completeness of Givental's 1996 argument was discussed in the literature
  (Lian-Liu-Yau; later expositions by Pandharipande (Bourbaki), Bini-De Concini-Polito-Procesi, Cox-Katz).
  These were not fetched here and no claim about their content is made; citing one complete account beside
  [MR1408320] would be a courtesy to the reader. The note already lists LLY97 in refs.bib (uncited).

### m3. Evaluating the formal J-function at Q = 1, t = T(q)H (minor)
Location: s02_setup.tex l.76-82 (thm:set-mirror), also thm:set-G0 (d) l.104 and a1_cyclicLR.tex l.13-17.
Problem: J(t,z) is defined as a formal series in t and the Novikov variable Q. Substituting t = T H with
T = log q + I~_1/C_0, whose term log q (the formal letter of def:set-genus0) is not topologically nilpotent,
