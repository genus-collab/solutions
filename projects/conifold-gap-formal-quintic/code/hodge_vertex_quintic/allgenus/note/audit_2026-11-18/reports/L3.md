## 1. Verdict in brief

The identification thm:mod-tr is **correct**, and it uses the quoted statements within their hypotheses. Every hypothesis of the
recalled theorem is verified for the model curve. Several of them are verified twice, through a citation and through a direct
computation, so the argument does not depend on the weakest of the quoted sentences.

- The model curve satisfies the hypotheses: it is compact (P^1), x is meromorphic, the branch points are simple, dy ≠ 0 there,
  the Bergman kernel is the standard one, and the critical values are distinct.
- The R-matrix is the formal Laplace transform (2-31) of the Bergman kernel. It equals M_0 in the correct index convention:
  prop:mod-Rtr, checked by hand and numerically to order t^3.
- (2-32), the condition relating y to R, holds: it is verified directly in prop:mod-Rtr (ii). It also follows from DNOPS
  Prop. 4.3 and from the sentence before DNOPS Thm 8.1.
- (2-33), the condition on B, is automatic for a compact curve with meromorphic dx, as DNOPS §2.6 states.
- The dilaton sign the manuscript takes from PPZ Def. 2.13 is the correct one:
  - DOSS (2-23) gives it.
  - It is forced by the unit axiom (2-11) of DNOPS's own definition of a CohFT.
  - My independent genus-1 computation with DNOPS's (2-28), (2-30)–(2-32) and (2-35) reproduces it, and excludes the printed
    DNOPS sign.
- The automorphism factors 1/|Aut Γ| and the normalisation Δ^{(2g−2+n)/2} of the TFT are those of DOSS (2-26), and the same
  computation confirms them.
- The factor 4 between W and f = 4W is handled correctly (Step 2 of the proof of thm:mod-tr, re-derived).
- The resulting CohFT is exactly Ω^mod_v of def:set-modcohft, with R-matrix M_0(z/ς^4), the TFT TFT^m_v, the factor (−5)^{h−1}
  and the dual forms ξ_a of def:mod-xi (Steps 3–4, re-derived).

All findings are **minor**: citation imprecisions, a notational mismatch in the parameters of (8-1), and two imprecisions in
DNOPS that the manuscript corrects silently or only in part. None affects validity.

## 3. Detailed notes

### 3.1 Hypotheses of the recalled theorem, and their verification for the model curve

The hypotheses of DNOPS §2.5–2.6 are:
- Σ is a Riemann surface; x and y are meromorphic on Σ.
- x has finitely many critical points c_i, used with local coordinates x = x(c_i) − w_i^2/2, so the critical points are simple.
- y is holomorphic near the c_i with non-vanishing derivative.
- B is symmetric, has a double pole on the diagonal with biresidue 1, and has no other singularities.
- The data are Δ from (2-30) and R^{−1} from (2-31).
- (2-32) holds.
- (2-33) holds, and it is "always satisfied if the curve is compact and dx is meromorphic".
- Then (2-35) holds, with {α_{g,k}} "the semi-simple CohFT determined by" Δ and R.
- DOSS Def. 3.1 also asks for distinct canonical coordinates a_i (the critical values).

For x_κ = κW_v and y = p on P^1 (v ≠ 0):
- dx_κ = κ(p^5−v)dp/p^2. Its zeros are the five simple points p^5 = v, with x'' = 5κς_b^2 ≠ 0. dx_κ has poles at 0 and ∞,
  which are not critical points.
- dy = dp ≠ 0.
- B = dp_1dp_2/(p_1−p_2)^2.
- The critical values (5/4)κζ^bς^4 are pairwise distinct.
- The curve is compact and dx is meromorphic, so (2-33) holds.

The manuscript's paraphrase in Step 1 of thm:mod-tr (s06_model_3.tex l. 23) replaces (2-33) by compactness, which is correct.
It adds symplecticity of R as a hypothesis. That is harmless: symplecticity is in any case implied by (2-33), and the manuscript
proves it independently in lem:set-M0sympl. The paraphrase attributes the compact-curve version to "DOSS Thm 4.1 as recalled in
DNOPS §2.6". Strictly, DOSS Thm 4.1 is purely local, and the global form is DNOPS's recollection, which follows LPSZ
(arXiv:1504.07439) and Eynard App. B. This is acceptable, and it is declared in (Q).

### 3.2 Normalisations: ħ, 1/|Aut|, the TFT, and the dilaton sign

- **ħ.**
  - DOSS: Z = exp Σħ^{g−1}F_g and Ω = Σ ω_{g,n} ħ^{g−1}. The genus power is the same on both sides.
  - DNOPS (2-35) has no ħ.
  - The manuscript's (−5)^{h−1} comes from the frozen basis, not from ħ.
- **1/|Aut|.**
  - DOSS (2-26) includes it.
  - PPZ Def. 2.2 includes it.
  - DNOPS §2.3 omits it (F3).
- **The TFT.** DNOPS §2.6 only says that the data "determine for us a semi-simple CohFT". The normalisation
  ω(e_j,…,e_j) = Δ_j^{(2g−2+n)/2} comes from DOSS (2-26) and (4-5): with h_1 = −1/(2√Δ), (−2h_1)^{2−2g−n} = Δ^{(2g−2+n)/2}.
  The manuscript cites the DOSS §2.3 graph weights, so this is covered.
- **The dilaton sign.**
  - DNOPS p. 9 prints the decoration of each dilaton leaf as −ψ(I − R^{−1}(ψ))e_1. PPZ Def. 2.13 has T(z) = z(1 − R^{−1}(z))1,
    so the printed sign is the opposite. The manuscript's remark after prop:mod-Rtr says exactly this, and is correct.
  - DOSS (2-23) gives −[z^{k−1}](R(−z))^i_1 for k ≥ 2 (Notation 2.6: dilaton labels are > 1). DOSS labels matrix entries with the
    upper index as the column (Remark 2.1, p. 6). The ordinary leaves (2-22) contract (R(−z))^i_j with v^{d,j}, and the dilaton
    leaves contract the same object with the unit. So, in terms of the leg operator L(z), the edge (2-24) is (1 − L(z)L(w)^T)/(z+w)
    and the dilaton vector is Σ_{k≥2}ψ^k(−[z^{k−1}]L(z)1) = ψ(1 − L(ψ))1. With L = R^{−1}_PPZ this is PPZ's translation.
  - The conversion from DOSS's marked-point dilaton leaves to the push-forward form is lem:set-pushins (ii). It is correct:
    ψ_jψ_{N+1} = π^*(ψ_j)ψ_{N+1}, and the translation is O(z^2).
- **Independent check of the sign** (`check_dilaton_sign.wl`). Data: one branch point, x = −w^2/2,
  B = dw_1dw_2/(w_1−w_2)^2 + β dw_1dw_2, y = aw + bw^3.
  - DNOPS (2-31) gives R^{−1}(z) = 1 − βz, so r_1 = β. (2-32) gives a = Δ^{−1/2} and b = −βa/3.
  - The kernel (2-28) gives ω_{1,1} = Δ^{1/2}[z^{−4}/8 + (13/24)β z^{−2}]dz + O(β^2).
  - The graph formula (2-35) gives the vertex term −β/24, the loop term +β/2 (with |Aut| = 2), the dilaton term ±β/24 and the
    descendant term (1/24)d(dξ/dx).
  - With the PPZ sign the two sides agree exactly. With the printed DNOPS sign they differ by −(1/12)βΔ^{1/2}z^{−2}.
  - A sign error in (2-31) relative to (2-35) would also show up: it gives 11/24 against −11/24.
  - So the combination the manuscript uses, DNOPS (2-28), (2-30)–(2-32), (2-35) with PPZ's action, is internally consistent at
    this order, and the printed DNOPS sign is a misprint.

### 3.3 The formal Laplace reading (lem:mod-laplace) and the "prescription" of DOSS §3.1, §3.5

- DOSS §3.1 (p. 12) defines topological recursion for germs given by power series (h^i_k, B^{ij}_{kl}). The integrals in §3.5
  (p. 22–23, (3-60)–(3-66)) are written over the paths where x − a_j ∈ ℝ^+. Neither section contains a stated rule for
  divergent or polar integrands. The coefficient formula (3-57), B̌_{d1,d2} = B_{2d1,2d2}(2d1−1)!!(2d2−1)!!, is the termwise
  Gaussian rule for even powers, and (3-64) asserts a value δ_{ij}(−1)^d u^d for the polar part.
- So "the prescription [DOSS §3.1, §3.5]" (s01_intro_3 l. 15) is a loose attribution (F2).
- The reading the manuscript uses is nevertheless forced.
  - Holomorphic parts: G_{2n} = (2n−1)!!ϑ^{−n} is Watson's lemma for the integral over the steepest-descent path.
  - The only polar term that occurs in (2-31) and (2-32) is w^{−2}dw, in dξ^i at its own branch point. dy is holomorphic there.
    Since w^{−2}e^{−ϑw^2/2} has zero residue, the integral over any path avoiding 0 is exactly −√(2πϑ). This is
    √(2π/ϑ)·G_{−2} with G_{−2} = −ϑ, and it is also the only value for which R^{−1} = 1 + O(ϑ^{−1}), as the group of DNOPS §2.3
    requires (R = I + O(z)).
  - G_{−2n} for n ≥ 2 and G_odd are never needed for a polar term.
  - Severity: minor.
- I checked lem:mod-laplace (i)–(iv) on monomials. For instance, in (i) mG_{m−1} = ϑG_{m+1} for m ≠ −2, and m = −1 gives
  ϑ = ϑ. In (iii) the scaling of w_j is k^2 and G_{±2s} scales by k^{±4s}. In (iv) both sides equal −n(2n−1)!!ϑ^{−n}.

### 3.4 prop:mod-Rtr: the R-matrix of the curve. Verified.

By hand, step by step:
- Step 1: ξ^i = −1/(√(−5κ)ς_i(q−ς_i)).
- Step 2: the partial fractions, −κ/√(−5κ) = √(−5κ)/5.
- Step 3:
  - (Mod2) from (i) with F = p^m, using −κϑ = 1/z.
  - (Mod1): from (iii) and (iv), 5v∂_vJ_m = −5(v/z)J_{m−1} + 5(ς_j^4/z)J_m, hence z∂_vJ_m = −J_{m−1} + J_m/ς_j.
  - The WKB exponent: ∂_v[W(ς_j(v))] = 1/ς_j.
  - Initial values: ε_bζ^{j(b+1)}.
  - Uniqueness through the pivot 4nζ^{4j}.
  - The table m_b = (0,−1,−2,2,1).
- Step 4: Ψ_0^{−1} = (1/5)ε_bζ^{−i(b+1)}.
- Step 5: inversion through M_0(−t)^T = M_0(t)^{−1}.
- (ii): Σ_k ζ^{−kb} = 5δ_{b0}.

Numerically (`check_Rtr.wl`, v = 1, κ = 1, 40 digits, orders t^0…t^3):
- −ϑ^{−1}L_{ς_j}[dξ^i], expanded through the local inverse series, equals M_0(t)_{ij} with t = −1/ϑ to about 10^{−35}.
- The transposed identification fails, with an error of 0.145. So the index placement matters and the manuscript's is the one that
  holds.
- M_0(−t)^T M_0(t) = 1 to 10^{−37}.
- Fs_0 = 1 − 3t/20 + 9t^2/800 + …, as stated in prop:set-modrows.

**Index convention.** DNOPS labels matrix entries with the upper index as the row (Remark 2.1, and Ψ on p. 7). Their legs
R^{−1}(ψ)e_α have the component (R^{−1})^j_α at a vertex of colour j. The edge (2-15) is
(I⊗I − R^{−1}⊗R^{−1})η^{αβ}e_α⊗e_β = (1 − R^{−1}(ψ')R^{−1}(ψ'')^T)/(ψ'+ψ''), which is PPZ Def. 2.2. So "upper index the row,
left action" is DNOPS's convention, and prop:mod-Rtr uses it.

I also checked this intrinsically. Match the positive-ϑ parts of the Laplace transforms at every branch point of the vertex forms
(Taylor coefficients of B at c_j) against the input forms d(d/dx)^dξ^i, using L_l[d(d/dx)^dξ^i] = (−ϑ)^d L_l[dξ^i]. The leg
(vertex j, input i) must then be the (j,i) entry of the matrix defined by (2-31), with the upper index the Laplace point. This is
the manuscript's reading. b_signs.tex l. 14 is consistent with it.

### 3.5 thm:mod-tr, Steps 1–4. Verified.

- **Step 1.** The data at κ = 4 are Δ^4_j = −20ς_j^2, ξ^{(4,j)} = −1/(√(−20)ς_j(p−ς_j)) and R^4(ψ) = M_0(ψ/(4ς^4)), all correct.
  dy/df = p^2/(4(p^5−v)) has no pole at 0 or ∞, so item (2) of Prop. 4.3 holds. Item (1) holds in its intended reading: y = p is
  the global coordinate and x(∞) = ∞.
- **Step 2 (the factor 4).** With √(−20) = 2√(−5):
  - ξ^4 = ξ^1/2, d/dx_4 = (1/4)d/dx_1, TFT^4 = 2^{2h−2+n}TFT^1, the unit is halved, and each dilaton leaf carries 2^{−1}.
  - Σ_v(2h_v−2+n_v) = 2h−2+n+#dil.
  - R(·/c) gives c^{−D}. For an edge this is c^{−1}E_R(ψ'/c, ψ''/c) of degree a+b+1. For a dilaton coefficient of z^m it is
    c^{−(m−1)}.
  - Total: 2^{2h−2+n}·4^{−(3h−3+n)}·2^{−n} = 4^{2−2h−n}, which matches lem:mod-scale (i) with κμ = 4 (the kernel scales by
    1/(κμ); re-derived).
  - This step is not even necessary: DNOPS §2.6 applies directly to the compact curve x = W_v, and prop:mod-Rtr verifies (2-32)
    at κ = 1. The sentence before Thm 8.1 is used only because (8-1) is monic.
- **Step 3.**
  - The frozen basis ē = e/√(−5) gives TFT^1(ē_j^n) = (−5)^{h−1}ς_j^{2h−2+n}, the metric −δ/5 and the unit 1̂.
  - The identification ē_j ↔ ẽ_j with Δ̂_j^{1/2} = ζ^{4j}ς = ς_j holds.
  - The rescaling lemma for λ^{h−1}Ω holds: the splitting axioms balance, and so does the unit (Ω'_{0,3}(v_1,v_2,1) = η'(v_1,v_2)).
    In R.(λ^{h−1}Ω) the edge picks up λ and Σ(h_v−1) + E = h−1.
- **Step 4.**
  - η(H^a,H^b) = 5δ_{a+b≡3}.
  - ξ'_a = −(ε_{3−a}/5)ς^{−ν_a−a}Σ_iς_i^a/(p−ς_i), with the partial fractions 5p^4/(p^5−v) and 5vp^{a−1}/(p^5−v).
  - ν_a + a = (0,0,5,5,5) and ε_{3−a} = (1,−1,−1,1,1) give exactly ξ_a of def:mod-xi, for all five a.
- **The resulting CohFT** is (Ψ^m_v)_*(M_0(z/ς^4).TFT^m_v) = Ω^mod_v of def:set-modcohft, with the unit-preserving action.
  Downstream, cor:mod-trF uses the flat unit H^0 through the dilaton equation, so it relies on the PPZ sign. That sign is the
  correct one (§3.2).
