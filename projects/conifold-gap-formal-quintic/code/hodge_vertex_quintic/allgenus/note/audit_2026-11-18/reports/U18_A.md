### m2 (minor, interpretive). What Corollary B is a statement about
- Where: s01_intro_2.tex l.29 and l.36; s10_main.tex l.75-81 (def:mai-Fcf); s10_main_2.tex l.1-7.
- F_g^cf is defined as C_{alpha,beta}(F_g^N) for the particular representative F_g^N in N of F_g. Since ev: N -> Q[[q]][l] is not proved injective, another element of N with the same evaluation F_g might a priori have a different conifold expansion; so "the conifold expansion of the genus-g potential" (intro l.29) is, as proved, the expansion of this representative, not an invariant of the function F_g. The authors disclose this (def:mai-Fcf: "Whether ev is injective on N is neither needed nor proved"; "What is not claimed" (3); sec:sgn-lit "Differences"). The statement of Corollary B is precise once def:mai-Fcf is read; the issue is only one of interpretation (in particular for the sentence in sec:sgn-lit l.106 that "the formal quintic has the conifold gap" in the frames of [CGYZ26]: their expansion is defined on functions, and its agreement with C(F_g^N) would need F_g^N, or another representative of F_g, to lie in their algebra).
- Possible repair (I did not need it, but it seems available): ev is injective. On Q[L^{+-1}] it is clear; for the jets, by Picard-Vessiot theory the transcendence degree over C(q) of the field generated by the 3-jets of two solutions spanning an isotropic plane equals the dimension 7 of the orbit of such pairs under the differential Galois group, which is Sp(4) for the quintic operator (Zariski density of the hypergeometric monodromy, Beukers-Heckman); the jets c_0..c_3, i_0..i_2 are 7 elements subject to no relation besides the concomitant one already used to eliminate i_3; and log q is transcendental over the Picard-Vessiot field, since Sp(4) has no quotient G_a. This would make F_g^cf an invariant of F_g, at the price of one more quoted input. Otherwise, the intro statement of Corollary B should say "of the graph-sum representative F_g^N".

### m3 (minor). Corollary B, Step 2: the justification of E_{R'} = E_X
