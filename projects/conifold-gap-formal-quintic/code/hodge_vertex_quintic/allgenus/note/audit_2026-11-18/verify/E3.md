# E3 - lem:fsp-res (iii): the exponential under X -> X + chi, as used in prop:con-res

VERDICT: real-minor. The earlier classification (U14 m4, U17 F3: minor) is right.
The last sentence of lem:fsp-res (iii) is true and suffices for prop:con-res, but the proof on the page
does not justify it: "ring homomorphism + equal to substitution on Laurent polynomials" does not by
itself determine the image of e^{-f_0/zh}, which is neither a Laurent polynomial nor u-adically small.
The missing step is a few lines of formal calculus (given below, Sections 3-4). It needs only
E' = g'E for E = e^{-f_0/zh}, g = -f_0/zh; no topology on R_res is needed.
Theorem P(b), the glue and Theorem C are unaffected.

## 2. What is on the page, exactly
lem:fsp-res (a_formalsp_5.tex l.86-95):
- R := R_res: sums sum_j c_j X^j, c_j in k((zh^{-1})), ord(c_j) >= eps|j| - M. (i): a
  k((zh^{-1}))-algebra containing k((zh^{-1}))[X^{+-1}] and exp(-f_0/zh), f_0 = P_+(X) + P_-(1/X);
  d/dX is a derivation. The proof of (i) (l.98) builds exp(-f_0/zh) as the product e_+ e_- of
  exp(-P_+(X)/zh) and exp(-P_-(1/X)/zh), each of which has finite-sum coefficients.
- (ii) convention: e^{-f/zh}Q := e^{-f_0/zh}(e^{-f_1/zh}Q), f = f_0 + f_1, f_1 in u k[X^{+-1}][[u]].
- (iii): Omega(X+chi) := sum_j Omega^{(j)} chi^j/j!, chi in u k[X^{+-1}][[u]];
  Res_X[Omega(X+chi)(1+chi')] = Res[Omega]; the map is a ring homomorphism, equal on Laurent
  polynomials to the substitution; "so for Omega = e^{-f(Y)/zh} beta(Y) it is e^{-f(X+chi)/zh}
  beta(X+chi), expanded termwise in u" (l.94).
- Proof (l.98): residue invariance via Omega(X+chi)(1+chi') = Omega + d/dX sum_{j>=1}
  Omega^{(j-1)} chi^j/j!; "multiplicative by the Leibniz rule ..., and it is the binomial expansion on
  X^{+-1}". Nothing is said about the exponential. That is the gap.

What is correct on the page (re-derived):
- Omega(X+chi) is well defined in R[[u]]: [u^N] is a finite sum over j <= N of products of elements of
  R with Laurent polynomials [u^b]chi^j in k[X^{+-1}].
- The derivative identity: d/dX of sum_{j>=1} Omega^{(j-1)}chi^j/j! is
  sum_{j>=1}Omega^{(j)}chi^j/j! + sum_{i>=0}Omega^{(i)}chi^i chi'/i! = (Omega(X+chi) - Omega) +
  Omega(X+chi)chi'. Termwise differentiation is legitimate (finite at each u-order). With (ii),
  Res_X[Omega(X+chi)(1+chi')] = Res[Omega]. Correct.
- Multiplicativity: Leibniz (Omega Psi)^{(n)} = sum binom(n,a) Omega^{(a)} Psi^{(n-a)} in R[[u]]
  (d/dX is a derivation of R by (i), coefficientwise in u), plus u-adic rearrangement. Correct.
- T := (Omega -> Omega(X+chi)) is additive, k((zh^{-1}))[[u]]-linear (d/dX kills these constants,
  coefficientwise in u) and u-adically continuous (T(u^N R[[u]]) in u^N R[[u]], since chi has u-order
  >= 1 and d/dX acts coefficientwise). T(X^{-1}) = sum_j (-1)^j X^{-1-j} chi^j = (X+chi)^{-1}.
  Hence T = sigma_chi (the u-adic Laurent substitution X -> X+chi) on k((zh^{-1}))[X^{+-1}][[u]]:
  both are continuous linear ring homomorphisms agreeing on X^{+-1}.

Why the ring-homomorphism argument is not enough: E = e^{-f_0/zh} lies in R but not in
k((zh^{-1}))[X^{+-1}][[u]], and E - 1 is not u-adically small; so neither "agreement on Laurent
polynomials" nor u-adic continuity says what T(E) is. Some extra input (a differential equation, or
continuity of T for the zh^{-1}-adic structure of R) is required. The referees' description of the
point is accurate.

## 3. The missing argument (complete)

Notation: R = R_res; Lambda' := k[zh^{-1}][X^{+-1}] (a subring of R); f_0 = P_+(X) + P_-(1/X) with
P_+-, in k[X] without constant term (as U17 F2 notes, constant coefficients are needed for (i); in the
only use f_0 = 1/X + X^4/4 over Q); g := -f_0/zh in Lambda'; E := e_+ e_- in R as in the proof of (i).

Step 1 (E' = g'E in R). Let p := -P_-(1/X)/zh, e_- = sum_k p^k/k!. The coefficient of X^{-j} in e_-
is a finite sum (k in [j/d, j]). Then [X^{-j-1}](p' e_-) = sum_k [X^{-j-1}](p' p^k)/k!
= sum_k [X^{-j-1}](p^{k+1})'/(k+1)! = [X^{-j-1}](e_-)', each sum finite (p' is a Laurent polynomial).
So e_-' = p' e_-; likewise e_+' = (-P_+/zh)' e_+. By Leibniz (d/dX a derivation of R, (i)),
E' = g'E. (This is also exactly what the proof of (ii) uses, unstated, for
d/dX(e^{-f/zh}Q) = e^{-f/zh}(Q' - zh^{-1}f'Q).)

Step 2 (higher derivatives). Let Y_j in Q[x_1,...,x_j] be the complete Bell polynomials,
G(t) := sum_j Y_j t^j/j! = exp(S), S := sum_{i>=1} x_i t^i/i!, in Q[x][[t]]. With the derivation
Dx_i = x_{i+1} of Q[x] one has dG/dt = (x_1 + DS)G = x_1 G + DG, i.e. Y_{j+1} = x_1 Y_j + D Y_j.
Let phi: Q[x] -> Lambda' be the ring homomorphism x_i -> g^{(i)}; then phi o D = (d/dX) o phi (both
are phi-derivations agreeing on the generators). By induction on j, E^{(j)} = phi(Y_j) E:
E^{(j+1)} = phi(Y_j)'E + phi(Y_j)g'E = phi(DY_j + x_1Y_j)E.

Step 3 (the exponential). Phi: Q[x][[t]] -> Lambda'[[u]], x_i -> g^{(i)}, t -> chi, is a well-defined
ring homomorphism (chi in u Lambda'[[u]], so sum_j p_j t^j -> sum_j phi(p_j) chi^j converges
u-adically) and maps t^m Q[x][[t]] into u^m Lambda'[[u]], so it commutes with exp on t Q[x][[t]].
Hence
  T(E) = sum_j E^{(j)} chi^j/j! = E * Phi(G) = E * exp(Phi(S)) = E * exp(sum_{i>=1} g^{(i)} chi^i/i!)
       = E * exp(T(g) - g) = e^{-f_0(X)/zh} * exp(-(f_0(X+chi) - f_0(X))/zh),
using T(g) = sigma_chi(g) = -f_0(X+chi)/zh (Section 2) and that multiplication by E is u-adically
continuous. Note f_0(X+chi) - f_0(X) lies in u k[X^{+-1}][[u]].

Step 4 (the u-small factors). T is u-adically continuous, k((zh^{-1}))[[u]]-linear and
multiplicative, so T(e^{-f_1/zh}) = sum_n T(-f_1/zh)^n/n! = e^{-f_1(X+chi)/zh}, with
f_1(X+chi) = sigma_chi(f_1) in u k[X^{+-1}][[u]]; and T(beta) = sigma_chi(beta) =: beta(X+chi) for
beta in k[zh^{+-1}][X^{+-1}][[u]].

Step 5 (conclusion). For Omega = e^{-f/zh}beta := E(e^{-f_1/zh}beta),
  Omega(X+chi) = T(E) T(e^{-f_1/zh}) T(beta)
              = e^{-f_0(X)/zh} * ( e^{-(f(X+chi) - f_0(X))/zh} beta(X+chi) ),
using exp(a)exp(b) = exp(a+b) for a, b in u Lambda'[[u]]. Since f(X+chi) = sigma_chi(f) has u^0-part
f_0(X) and f(X+chi) - f_0(X) in u k[X^{+-1}][[u]], this is e^{-f(X+chi)/zh}beta(X+chi) in the
convention of (ii). This is the last sentence of (iii), now proved.

Alternatives (both checked): (a) ODE in R[[eps]]: T_eps(h) := sum_j h^{(j)}eps^j/j! satisfies
d/deps T_eps(h) = T_eps(h'); T_eps(E) and E exp(T_eps(g) - g) both solve Z' = T_eps(g')Z with
Z(0) = E; the recursion (n+1)Z_{n+1} = sum_i T_eps(g')_i Z_{n-i} has a unique solution (char 0); then
substitute eps -> chi (ring homomorphism R[[eps]] -> R[[u]], continuous). This is U17's closure;
U14's version with eps -> 1 also works on the subring where the eps^j-coefficient has u-order >= j.
(b) zh^{-1}-adic continuity: with nu(Omega) := inf_j ord(c_j), nu is >= nu under d/dX and under
multiplication by elements of k[X^{+-1}]; [u^N]T(Omega) = sum_{j<=N} Omega^{(j)} [u^N]chi^j/j! for
u-constant Omega, so nu([u^N]T(E - E_K)) >= K+1 for the partial sums E_K of sum_k g^k/k! (which
equal E coefficientwise, a rearrangement of the e_+ e_- product); T(E_K) is a Laurent substitution;
let K -> infinity.

## 4. The exact use in prop:con-res (s08_connection_7.tex l.24-28)
Apply (iii) with the variable Y renamed: Omega(Y) := e^{-W_V(Y)/zh} a_Y(Y), where
- f = W_V = V/Y + Y^4/4 = f_0 + f_1, f_0 = 1/Y + Y^4/4 (P_+ = Y^4/4, P_- = 1/Y, constant
  coefficients, no constant term), f_1 = (V-1)/Y in u Q[Y^{-1}][[u]] since V in 1 + uQ[[u]]
  (lem:con-cfu);
- beta = a_Y in Q[Y, zh][[u]] (lem:con-red), a subset of k[zh^{+-1}][Y^{+-1}][[u]];
- chi := Y(X) - X = X sum_k u^k Y_k(X^5) in u Q[X][[u]] (lem:con-cfu). Hypotheses of (iii) hold.
Then:
- f(X+chi) = sigma_chi(W_V) = V/Y(X) + Y(X)^4/4 = hatW(X) in Q[X^{+-1}][[u]] (lem:con-cfu; there
  1/Y is the u-adic expansion X^{-1}(1 + w)^{-1}, w = chi/X, which equals sigma_chi(Y^{-1}) =
  sum_j (-1)^j X^{-1-j}chi^j). Its u^0-part is 1/X + X^4/4 (lem:con-form (c)), the same f_0.
- beta(X+chi)(1+chi') = sigma_chi(a_Y) Y'(X) = hat a(X): a_Y = sigma_{X(Y)}(hat a) X'(Y);
  sigma_chi o sigma_{X(Y)} = sigma_{X(Y(X))} = id (composition of continuous Laurent substitutions,
  determined on X^{+-1}; X(Y(X)) = X by the inverse map of lem:con-cfu), and the chain rule
  d/dX sigma_chi(h) = sigma_chi(h') Y'(X) (both sides sigma_chi-derivations agreeing on X) applied to
  X(Y(X)) = X gives sigma_chi(X'(Y)) Y'(X) = 1. Routine.
- So, by Step 5, Omega(X+chi)(1+chi') = e^{-(1/X + X^4/4)/zh}(e^{-(hatW - 1/X - X^4/4)/zh} hat a),
  which is exactly the element of R_res[[u]] whose residue is hatV in lem:con-relab (which reads
  e^{-hatW/zh} with the (ii) convention: "at each u-order the integrand is e^{-(1/X+X^4/4)/zh} times
  a Laurent polynomial"). Hence Res_Y[e^{-W_V/zh} a_Y dY] = Res_X[e^{-hatW/zh} hat a dX] = hatV, the
  first equality of the display in prop:con-res. The exact part vanishes by (ii) with Q = calE in
  Y Q[Y, zh][[u]], using Step 1 for E'. Linearity over Q[zh][[u]] (pi_m) is clear (Res is
  k((zh^{-1}))-linear and coefficientwise in u).
The use in prop:con-res is therefore covered exactly; nothing stronger than the repaired (iii) is used.
Downstream: thm:con-Pb Step 6 (and Steps 1-4), the glue (thm:con-glue) and Theorem C inherit nothing
else from (iii). No effect.

## 7. Proposed repair text (replace the last sentence of the proof of (iii))
"For E := e^{-f_0/zh} and g := -f_0/zh one has E' = g'E (the coefficients of e^{-P_+-/zh} are finite
sums). By induction E^{(j)} = Y_j(g',...,g^{(j)})E, Y_j the complete Bell polynomials,
sum_j Y_j(x)t^j/j! = exp(sum_{i>=1} x_i t^i/i!), since Y_{j+1} = x_1Y_j + DY_j with Dx_i = x_{i+1}.
The substitution x_i -> g^{(i)}, t -> chi is a u-adically continuous ring homomorphism
Q[x][[t]] -> k[zh^{-1}][X^{+-1}][[u]], so E(X+chi) = E exp(sum_{i>=1} g^{(i)}chi^i/i!) =
e^{-f_0(X)/zh}e^{-(f_0(X+chi)-f_0(X))/zh}. The map Omega -> Omega(X+chi) is u-adically continuous and
k((zh^{-1}))[[u]]-linear, so it sends e^{-f_1/zh} to e^{-f_1(X+chi)/zh}; by multiplicativity,
Omega(X+chi) = e^{-f_0(X)/zh}(e^{-(f(X+chi)-f_0(X))/zh}beta(X+chi)), which is e^{-f(X+chi)/zh}
beta(X+chi) in the convention of (ii)."
