\subsection{The setting}
\label{sec:int-setting}

Let the torus $(\bbC^*)^5$ act on $\bbP^4$ with weights $\lambda_0,\dots,\lambda_4$ on $H^0(\bbP^4,\cO(1))$. The \emph{formal quintic} of Lho and Pandharipande \cite[\S0.2, (5)]{LP18}, \cite[\S0.2, (5)]{LP20} is the $\cO(5)$-twisted equivariant Gromov--Witten theory of $\bbP^4$ at the specialisation $\lambda_i=\zeta^i$, $\zeta=\re^{2\pi\ri/5}$, of \cite{ZZ08} (see \cite[\S3.1, (9)]{GJR18}): its invariants are $N_{g,d}=\int_{[\overline{\mathcal M}_g(\bbP^4,d)]^{\mathrm{vir}}}e(R\pi_*f^*\cO(5))$, the Euler class being defined by virtual localisation \cite{MR1666787} before the weights are specialised, and its genus-$g$ potential is $F_g=\sum_{d\geq0}N_{g,d}\Qn^d$, the constant maps included (\cref{def:set-fq}). The genus-zero theory at this specialisation is governed by the hypergeometric series $C_0(q)=\sum_{d\geq0}\frac{(5d)!}{(d!)^5}q^d$ of the quintic threefold: by the genus-zero mirror theorem the $J$-function at $t=T(q)H$ is $I(q,z)/C_0(q)$, with the mirror map $T=I_1/C_0$ (\cref{thm:set-mirror}), and $F_g$ becomes a function of $q$ through $\Qn=\re^{T(q)}$. In the flat basis $1,H,\dots,H^4$ the quantum product is cyclic, $H*H^a=\kappa^{\mathrm{LR}}_aH^{a+1}$ (\cref{prop:set-cyclicLR}). With $\Dq=q\,\dd/\dd q$, $C_1=\Dq T$, $u:=1-5^5q$ and $L:=u^{-1/5}=(1-5^5q)^{-1/5}$, the canonical coordinates satisfy $\Dq u_i=\zeta^iL$ and the normalising factors of the idempotents are $\Delta_i=C_0^2\zeta^{3i}/L^2$ (\cref{cor:set-cyclicPF5}). We write $s:=u^{1/5}=1/L$; the large-radius point $q=0$ is $u=1$, and $u=0$ is the \emph{conifold point} (\cref{def:set-points}). There the five idempotents degenerate together: in the conifold expansion below, $\Delta_i^{1/2}=\zeta^{4i}C_0/L$ becomes $\zeta^{4i}s\varpi_0$, which vanishes at $s=0$ (\cref{def:set-hat}).

The formal quintic is a semisimple cohomological field theory, and by Teleman's reconstruction \cite[Theorem~(2) in (1.15)]{MR2917177}\draftnote{numbers from arXiv v3; journal numbering not compared} (see \cite[\S4.1, Theorem~4.1]{GJR18}) its genus-$g$ potential is the Givental--Teleman sum over the stable graphs of genus $g$ of its $R$-matrix acting on its topological field theory $\TFT$, in the convention of \cite[Defs.~2.2, 2.13]{PPZ15} (\cref{def:set-graphsum,thm:set-GT}). Here $R(z)=1+O(z)$ is Givental's $R$-matrix: for the normalised idempotents $\tilde e_i$ the columns $R(z)\tilde e_i\,\re^{u_i/z}$ solve the quantum differential equation, and $R$ is symplectic (\cref{def:set-rows}). At the large-radius point its value is given by quantum Riemann--Roch \cite[\S2.2, Theorem~1]{Giv01}, \cite[Corollary~1]{MR2276766}, \cite[(17)]{GJR18}:
\[
R(q=0)=\diag\bigl(h_{\bw_0},\dots,h_{\bw_4}\bigr),\qquad h_{\bw}(z)=\exp\Bigl(-\sum_{k\geq1}\bk_k\,z^{2k-1}\sum_{a\in\bw}a^{1-2k}\Bigr),\qquad \bk_k=\frac{B_{2k}}{2k(2k-1)},
\]
with $\bw_i=\{\zeta^i-\zeta^j\}_{j\neq i}\cup\{-5\zeta^i\}$; this is the input (QRR) of \cref{sec:int-inputs} (\cref{thm:set-qrr}), and we write $h:=h_{\bw_0}$. Near the conifold point the rows grow with $L$: for the lowered rows $\Qr^{(i)}_a(z)=\eta(H^a,R(z)\tilde e_i)$, the coefficient $A_n=\coef{z^n}(C_0\Qr^{(0)}_0)$ is a polynomial in $L$ of degree at most $4n+1$ (\cref{def:set-rows}; by \cref{thm:gau-R0}~(ii)--(iii) it is a combination of the canonical amplitudes $A^{\mathrm{can}}_m$, $m\leq n$, to which \cref{prop:con-deg} applies), and the natural variable there is $\zh:=L^4z=z/s^4$ (\cref{def:set-hat}).

\emph{Conifold frames.} Let $f_0,f_1,f_2$ be the solutions $f_k=u^k+O(u^3)$ at $u=0$ of the Picard--Fuchs operator $\PFfour$ of the quintic, and $\varpi_1=f_1+\frac7{10}f_2=u+O(u^2)$ the vanishing period, the coefficient of $\log u$ in the logarithmic solution (\cref{lem:set-frobbasis}). A \emph{conifold frame} is a period $\varpi_0=f_0+\alpha\varpi_1+\beta f_2$ with $(\alpha,\beta)\in\bbQ^2$, and $\tc:=\varpi_1/\varpi_0=u+O(u^2)$ is the flat coordinate of the vanishing period (\cref{def:set-frames}). The coefficients of the rows of $R$ lie in $\Nring\tensor\bbQ(\zeta)$, where $\Nring$ is a differential ring generated by the periods, $L^{\pm1}$ and a formal logarithm of $q$ (\cref{def:set-confexp,cor:gau-rowsNN}), and the \emph{conifold expansion} $\Cexp_{\alpha,\beta}\colon\Nring\to\bbQ\lser{s}$ expands its elements at $u=0$ in the frame $(\alpha,\beta)$; an expanded quantity is \emph{regular at the conifold point} if it lies in $\bbQ\fps{u}$.

\emph{The model and the natural form.} The model is the Landau--Ginzburg potential $\Wv(p)=p^4/4+v/p$ with primitive form $\dd p$; its five critical points $p^5=v$ coalesce, as $v\to0$, into the simple pole at $p=0$ (\cref{def:set-model}). Its rows are, up to signs, the normalised formal stationary-phase series of $\int p^{\nu_b}\re^{-\Wv(p)/z}\dd p$, $\nu=(0,-1,3,2,1)$, at the critical points (\cref{prop:set-modrows,lem:con-S2}); they depend on $z$ through $t=z/\varsigma^4$, $\varsigma=v^{1/5}$, and in Teleman's normalisation they form the model $R$-matrix $\Mo(t)$ (\cref{def:set-M0}), which is symplectic (\cref{lem:set-M0sympl}). Let
\[
\tau(u):=\frac{1}{{}_2F_1\bigl(1,\frac45;\frac95;u\bigr)}=\Bigl(\sum_{n\geq0}\frac{4u^n}{5n+4}\Bigr)^{-1},\qquad v(u):=u\,\tau(u)^{-5/4}\qquad\text{(\cref{def:set-tau})}.
\]
At $v=v(u)$ the canonical coordinates of the model are those of the formal quintic measured from the conifold point, and $z/\varsigma^4=\tau(u)\zh=-\frac54\zeta^iz/(u_i-u_i^{\mathrm c})$ (\cref{lem:set-canon,prop:gau-equalprod}). So $M:=\Mo(\tau(u)\zh)$ is the $R$-matrix of the model at the point $v(u)$, written in the variable of the formal quintic. Writing the matrix $\Qr$ of the lowered rows of $R$, expanded in a conifold frame, as $\Qr=R'M$ with $R':=\Qr M^{-1}$ gives the \emph{natural form} of the graph sum: the outer graph sum $R'.(M.\TFT)$ has the vertices $M.\TFT$ and the edges and leaves of $R'$ (\cref{def:set-outer}).

The shape of this factorisation is that of a Hodge factorisation: $R$ is written as a gauge that is regular at the singular point times the $R$-matrix of a universal local model in its natural variable. For the formal quintic the local model is $\Wv$, and its natural variable is $z$ divided by the canonical coordinate measured from the conifold point, which degenerates there and therefore depends on $u$; the counterpart of a Hodge factor is $\Mo(\tau(u)\zh)$ together with the regular gauge (\cref{rmk:mai-analogy}). Frames of Hodge type do not suffice here, at least at genus $2$: the computations at genus $2$ recorded in \cref{sec:evi-hodge} show that, for each of the frames considered there in which the $R$-matrix is multiplied by $q$-independent factors of Hodge type, every coloured graph with an edge remains singular at the conifold point.

\subsection{The conifold gap}
\label{sec:int-gap}

The conifold gap is a prediction of the topological B-model: near a conifold point the genus-$g$ potential, expanded in the flat coordinate given by the vanishing period, has the universal leading pole of the $c=1$ string at the self-dual radius and no other pole \cite{Bershadsky:1993cx} (see \cite[\S1]{HKQ09}), \cite{Ghoshal:1995wm}, \cite[\S2.3]{HK07}, \cite[(1.2)]{HKQ09}. For the quintic threefold it is the one of the four structural predictions listed in the abstract of \cite{GJR18} that remained open after \cite{GJR18,CGL21,CGL26} (see \cite[Remark~1.6]{GJR18}), and it is an input of the computations of invariants of high genus \cite{HKQ09,LR22}. For the resolved conifold it follows from the multiple-cover formula \cite[\S1.1, footnote~1]{Br25}; it was proved for local $\bbP^2$ in all genera in \cite{Br25}, after a check to genus $7$ in \cite{CI21} (as reported in \cite[\S1.2]{Br25}); a conifold gap theorem for the topological recursion of toric mirror curves at a generic one-node degeneration is proved in \cite{FCCZ26}; and the conifold gap of the quintic threefold is proved in \cite[Theorem~2]{CGYZ26}, by master-space localisation.

The potentials of the formal quintic lie in a finitely generated ring and satisfy holomorphic anomaly equations of the form conjectured for the quintic \cite[Theorems~1 and~2]{LP20}; these equations determine them up to finitely many constants of integration, which are computable by localisation \cite[\S0.4]{LP20}. The formal quintic enters the approach of Guo, Janda and Ruan to the gap of the quintic \cite{GJR18}: by \cite[Remark~1.9]{CGYZ26}, that approach reduces the conifold gap of the quintic to two key statements, the conifold gap for the formal quintic and the conifold regularity of its correlators with special insertions, and the authors of \cite{CGYZ26} expect their method to prove the conifold gap for the formal quintic as well (loc.\ cit.).

We prove, conditionally on the inputs of \cref{sec:int-inputs}, a graph-local statement for the formal quintic (Theorem~A below). Summing over the graphs gives the conifold gap for the formal quintic in every genus $g\geq2$ (Corollary~B), with a constant $c_g$ that agrees with the constant of \cite[Conjecture~1.5]{CGYZ26} for the quintic in the same normalisation of the vanishing period (\cref{rmk:int-constant}).

Near the conifold point the $R$-matrix of the Givental--Teleman sum, normalised at $q=0$ by quantum Riemann--Roch, factorises through the $R$-matrix of the Landau--Ginzburg model $\Wv$ with primitive form $\dd p$. The correlators of the model are computed by topological recursion on the model curve $x=\Wv(p)$, $y=p$, through \cite[Theorem~4.1]{DuninBarkowski:2012bw} as recalled in \cite[\S2.6]{Dunin-Barkowski:2015caa}, applied to $4\Wv$, which is the same curve up to scaling, together with \cite{Eynard:2007kz} and \cite[Theorem~7.1]{ABDKS25}; topological recursion is applied to this curve only, never to a mirror curve of the quintic. The proof of \cite{CGYZ26} also proceeds through a factorisation of an $R$-matrix, that of a master-space theory, $R=R^{\mathrm{reg}}R^{\mathrm{prin}}$ \cite[Proposition~3.1]{CGYZ26}; here it is the $R$-matrix of the formal quintic itself that is factorised, through a universal local model. \draftnote{The authors decide whether to cite their companion notes and how to relate the note to the sentence of \cite[\S1.1]{Br25} on $R$-matrix methods.}

The normalisation at $q=0$ is carried to the conifold point by an identity of formal stationary phase for a Mellin--Barnes integral (Theorem~Gamma below). Its mechanism, a change of chart in which the critical point survives at $q=0$ and the integrand there factorises into Euler integrals, is the one of Givental's treatment of projective spaces \cite[\S10]{MR1901075}. The analogous identity for the Landau--Ginzburg integral of Guo, Janda and Ruan is stated without proof in \cite[Remark~7.2]{GJR18}, and the value at the orbifold point is \cite[Lemma~7.7, (37)]{GJR18}. For Gamma factors in quantum cohomology see also \cite[Lemma~4.20]{MR2553377} and \cite{GGI16}. \draftnote{Re-check the latest versions of arXiv:2609.13921 and arXiv:2509.19298 before submission.}
