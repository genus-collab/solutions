\section{The model: topological recursion on the model curve}
\label{sec:mod}

Topological recursion is applied in this section, and only here, to the curve $x=\Wv(p)$, $y=p$ on $\bbP^1$. The theorem of \cite{DuninBarkowski:2012bw} that identifies topological recursion with a Givental--Teleman graph sum is applied to $x=4\Wv$, which is the same curve up to scaling, and transferred to $x=\Wv$ by \cref{lem:mod-scale}; the closed formula of \cite[Thm.~7.1]{ABDKS25} is applied to $x=\Wv$ itself. Nothing about the quintic enters, and results that the literature states for families of curves are used only for the model curves $x=\Wv$, $v\neq 0$. The section proves the Model Lemma on $\sK$ in every genus (\cref{thm:mod-MLK}). The argument runs as follows: the model CohFT $\Omega^{\mathrm{mod}}_v$ of \cref{def:set-modcohft} is topological recursion on the model curve (\cref{thm:mod-tr}); insertions in $\sK$ become legs, that is functions on the curve, through a leg dictionary (\cref{prop:mod-LD}), and the logarithmic part of a leg is computed by the vanishing tower (\cref{prop:mod-dii}); the legs of $\sKo$ form an explicit lattice (\cref{thm:mod-lattice}); the pairings of legs with the correlators are controlled by L-regularity (\cref{thm:mod-Lreg}), by the Gap Theorem (\cref{thm:mod-gap}) and by Theorem~V on logarithmic legs (\cref{thm:mod-V}).

The quoted inputs (Q) of \cref{sec:int-inputs} that enter here are: \cite[Def.~4.2 (4-9) with the kernel (4-3), Thm.~4.2, Thm.~4.6, Thm.~5.1 (5-14) with \S5.3 (5-27)--(5-30)]{Eynard:2007kz}; \cite[Thm.~4.1]{DuninBarkowski:2012bw} with its graph weights \cite[\S2.3]{DuninBarkowski:2012bw}, as recalled in \cite[\S2.6, (2-30)--(2-35)]{Dunin-Barkowski:2015caa}, with the action of \cite[\S2.3]{Dunin-Barkowski:2015caa} (its dilaton leaves taken with the sign of \cite[Def.~2.13]{PPZ15}), the compatibility condition \cite[Prop.~4.3 (1)]{Dunin-Barkowski:2015caa} and the sentence preceding \cite[Thm.~8.1]{Dunin-Barkowski:2015caa}, applied to $f=4\Wv$, that is to \cite[(8-1)]{Dunin-Barkowski:2015caa} with $n=4$ and $a=(0,0,0,0,4v)$; and \cite[Thm.~7.1: (7.6), (7.7), (7.9), (7.10), with the kernel (1.1)]{ABDKS25}. The action of $R$-matrices is that of \cite[Defs.~2.2, 2.5, 2.13]{PPZ15} (\cref{def:set-graphsum}). Two further statements of \cite{Eynard:2007kz}, not among the inputs (Q), the dilaton equation \cite[Thm.~4.7 (4-30)]{Eynard:2007kz} and the variation (5-15) of the free energies in \cite[Thm.~5.1]{Eynard:2007kz}, are recalled in \cref{cor:mod-trF} and in \cref{thm:mod-var}~(ii), but no proof uses them. Every other step is proved here.

\subsection{Conventions; the model as topological recursion}
\label{sec:mod-tr}

\begin{conv}[Topological recursion]
\label{conv:mod-kernel}
Let $x,y$ be meromorphic functions on $\bbP^1$ such that $\dd x$ has only simple zeros, the \emph{branch points} $\varsigma_b$, and $\dd y\neq 0$ there; let $\iota_b$ be the local involution of $x$ near $\varsigma_b$ ($x\circ\iota_b=x$, $\iota_b\neq\mathrm{id}$). Put $\omega_{0,1}:=y\,\dd x$ and $\omega_{0,2}(p_1,p_2):=\dd p_1\,\dd p_2/(p_1-p_2)^2$. For $2h-2+n+1>0$ the correlators $\omega_{h,n+1}$ are defined by the recursion, with $J=(p_1,\dots,p_n)$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mod-kernel}
\begin{gathered}
\omega_{h,n+1}(p_0,J)=\sum_b\Res_{q\to\varsigma_b}K_b(p_0,q)\Big[\omega_{h-1,n+2}(q,\iota_bq,J)+{\sum}'\,\omega_{h_1,|I_1|+1}(q,I_1)\,\omega_{h_2,|I_2|+1}(\iota_bq,I_2)\Big],\\
K_b(p_0,q):=\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\int_{\iota_bq}^{q}\omega_{0,2}(p_0,\cdot)}{\big(y(q)-y(\iota_bq)\big)\,\dd x(q)},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
where ${\sum}'$ runs over $h_1+h_2=h$ and $I_1\sqcup I_2=J$ with the two terms containing $\omega_{0,1}$ omitted. Here $q$ is a point of the curve (the parameter $q$ of the formal quintic does not occur in this section). For $h\geq 2$ the free energy is defined by $(2-2h)\FTR_h:=\sum_b\Res_{\varsigma_b}\Phi_{y\dd x}\,\omega_{h,1}$, where $\dd\Phi_{y\dd x}=y\,\dd x$. This is the convention of \cite[(2-26)--(2-29)]{Dunin-Barkowski:2015caa} and of \cite[(2.1)]{Hock25}. The kernel printed in \cite[(4-3), (4-9)]{Eynard:2007kz} equals the kernel of \cite[(1.1)]{ABDKS25} with $\omega^{(0)}_{1,0}=-y\,\dd x$, and both are $-K_b$. Since $\omega_{h,n}$ is built with $2h-2+n$ kernels, the correlators $W^{(h)}_n$ of \cite{Eynard:2007kz} and $\omega^{(h)}_{n,0}$ of \cite{ABDKS25} are
\[
\omega_{h,n}=(-1)^nW^{(h)}_n=(-1)^n\omega^{(h)}_{n,0},\qquad \FTR_h=-F^{(h)},
\]
with $F^{(h)}$ of \cite[Def.~4.3 (4-10)]{Eynard:2007kz}; in particular our $\omega_{0,3}$ is the negative of \cite[Thm.~4.1 (4-20)]{Eynard:2007kz}. The variational formula is translated in \cref{thm:mod-var}, and the Laplace integrals of \cite[(2-31), (2-32)]{Dunin-Barkowski:2015caa} are read as formal Laplace series (\cref{lem:mod-laplace}). The same dictionary is recorded in \cref{sec:sgn-tr} (\cref{tab:sgn-tr}). For orientation only (classical; used in no proof): with this convention the curve $x=-p^2/2$, $y=p$ gives $\omega_{h,n}=\sum\langle\tau_{d_1}\cdots\tau_{d_n}\rangle_h\prod_l(2d_l+1)!!\,\dd p_l/p_l^{2d_l+2}$, the intersection numbers of $\psi$ classes, and $x=p+1/p$, $y=p$ gives $\FTR_2=-1/240$.
\end{conv}

By \cite[Thm.~4.2, Thm.~4.6]{Eynard:2007kz}, translated by the dictionary above, every $\omega_{h,n}$ with $2h-2+n>0$ is a symmetric meromorphic multidifferential whose poles, in each variable, lie at the branch points only; in particular it has no poles on the diagonals.

\begin{defn}[The model curve]
\label{def:mod-curve}
Let $v\in\bbC$, $v\neq 0$, fix a fifth root $\varsigma$ of $v$, and let $x:=\Wv(p)=p^4/4+v/p$ and $y:=p$ on $\bbP^1$ with the coordinate $p$. Then $\dd x=(p^5-v)\,\dd p/p^2$; the branch points are $\varsigma_b:=\zeta^{-b}\varsigma$, $b\in\bbZ/5$; $x''(\varsigma_b)=5\varsigma_b^2\neq 0$; the critical values $x(\varsigma_b)=\frac54\zeta^b\varsigma^4$ are pairwise distinct; $\dd y=\dd p\neq 0$; and $p=0$, $p=\infty$ are the poles of $x$ and of $y\,\dd x$. The five points of a fibre $x^{-1}(X)$ are the roots of $T^5-4XT+4v$. The weights are $p:1$, $v:5$, $x:4$, $\bar p:=v/p:4$, $\log p:0$. The correlators $\omega_{h,n}$ and $\FTR_h$ of this curve are those of \cref{conv:mod-kernel}; their homogeneity in $v$ is \cref{lem:mod-scale}~(ii). Other normalisations of the model curve appear only in \cref{sec:sgn-model} (\cref{tab:sgn-model}).
\end{defn}

\begin{defn}[Pairings with legs]
\label{def:mod-pair}
The \emph{leg class} is $\cL:=\bbQ[v][p]+\bbQ(v)[x]$. For $2h-2+n>0$ and legs $\phi_1,\dots,\phi_n\in\cL$ put
\[
\langle\phi_1\cdots\phi_n\rangle^{\mathrm{TR}}_h:=\sum_{b_1,\dots,b_n}\Res_{p_1=\varsigma_{b_1}}\cdots\Res_{p_n=\varsigma_{b_n}}\prod_{l=1}^n\phi_l(p_l)\,\omega_{h,n}(p_1,\dots,p_n),
\]
the \emph{residue pairing}; for one slot we write $\langle\phi,w\rangle:=\sum_b\Res_{\varsigma_b}\phi\,w$. For legs in $\bbQ[v][p]$ the residue pairing equals the pairing $(2\pi\ri)^{-1}\oint_{|p|=R}$ on a circle with $R>|\varsigma|$, since such a leg is holomorphic at $p=0$, where $\omega_{h,n}$ is holomorphic too. For a leg with a pole at $p=0$ (for instance $f(x)$ with $f$ a polynomial of positive degree) the circle pairing differs from the residue pairing by the residue at $p=0$, which is not zero in general (an example is computed in \cref{sec:evi-model}; not used). Let $\mathfrak r$ be a ray from $0$ to $\infty$ avoiding the branch points. The \emph{log leg} $-\log p$ is paired by
\[
\langle\cdots,-\log p,\cdots\rangle^{\mathrm{TR}}_h:=\int_{p'\in\mathfrak r}\omega_{h,n}(\dots,p',\dots),
\]
and several log legs by iterated integrals along rays; in a mixed pairing the slots with legs in $\cL$ are paired by residues and the other slots along rays. These integrals converge and do not depend on the rays: in each variable $\omega_{h,n}$ has poles only at the branch points (the paragraph after \cref{conv:mod-kernel}), so it is holomorphic at $0$ and at $\infty$; its residues at the branch points vanish (\cref{lem:mod-xfun} with $f=1$, proved below; its proof does not use pairings); and moving a ray across $\varsigma_b$ changes the integral by $2\pi\ri$ times such a residue, which vanishes. Moreover, if $\eta$ is a meromorphic $1$-form on $\bbP^1$ with poles only at the branch points and zero residues there, then $\eta=\dd G$ with $G$ rational and holomorphic at $0$ and $\infty$, and
\[
\int_{\mathfrak r}\eta=G(\infty)-G(0)=\sum_b\Res_{\varsigma_b}(-\log p)\,\eta,
\]
for any branch of $\log p$ near each $\varsigma_b$: indeed $\Res_{\varsigma_b}(\log p\,\dd G)=-\Res_{\varsigma_b}G\,\dd p/p$, and the residue theorem for the rational form $G\,\dd p/p$, whose other poles are $0$ and $\infty$, gives $-\sum_b\Res_{\varsigma_b}G\,\dd p/p=G(0)-G(\infty)$. So a log leg may equally be paired by residues at the branch points.
\end{defn}

\begin{defn}[Dual descendant functions]
\label{def:mod-xi}
Let $D_x:=\dd/\dd x=\frac{p^2}{p^5-v}\frac{\dd}{\dd p}$ acting on functions, and for $a\in\bbZ/5$
\[
\xi_a:=\varepsilon'_a\,v^{\alpha_a}\frac{p^{m_a}}{p^5-v},\quad (m_a,\varepsilon'_a,\alpha_a)=(4,-1,0),\ (0,1,1),\ (1,1,0),\ (2,-1,0),\ (3,-1,0)\quad(a=0,\dots,4),
\]
and $\xi_{a,d}:=D_x^d\xi_a$ for $d\geq 0$. (The signs $\varepsilon'_a$ are local to this section; they are not the signs $\varepsilon_b$ of \cref{def:set-model}.) Each $\xi_{a,d}$ is rational with poles only at the branch points, holomorphic at $p=0$, and $O(p^{-1-4d})$ at $p=\infty$.
\end{defn}

\begin{defn}[Ancestor vector of a leg]
\label{def:mod-ancvec}
For a leg $\phi\in\cL$, or $\phi=-\log p$, or a $\bbQ[v]$-linear combination of these, put $q_\phi(z):=\sum_{a,d}\langle\phi,\dd\xi_{a,d}\rangle H^az^d$; a leg $\phi$ with $q_\phi=X$ is called a \emph{TR leg} of $X$. Here $\langle-\log p,\dd\xi_{a,d}\rangle=\xi_{a,d}(\infty)-\xi_{a,d}(0)$ by \cref{def:mod-pair}.
\end{defn}

The ancestor correlators $\langle\prod_i\psi^{d_i}H^{a_i}\rangle_h(v)$ of $\Omega^{\mathrm{mod}}_v$ are those of \cref{def:set-modcohft}, extended multilinearly to vectors $q_1,\dots,q_n\in\bbH[z]$ (\cref{conv:tra-forms}).

\begin{thm}[The model as topological recursion]
\label{thm:mod-tr}
Let $v\neq 0$ and $2h-2+n>0$. The correlators of the model curve are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mod-tr}
\omega_{h,n}(p_1,\dots,p_n)=(-5)^{h-1}\sum_{a_1,\dots,a_n}\sum_{d_1,\dots,d_n\geq 0}\Big\langle\prod_{i=1}^n\psi^{d_i}H^{a_i}\Big\rangle_h(v)\,\prod_{i=1}^n\dd\xi_{a_i,d_i}(p_i),
\end{equation}
a finite sum, the brackets being the ancestor correlators of $\Omega^{\mathrm{mod}}_v$. Equivalently, for legs $\phi_1,\dots,\phi_n$ as in \cref{def:mod-ancvec}, $\langle q_{\phi_1}\cdots q_{\phi_n}\rangle_h=(-5)^{1-h}\langle\phi_1\cdots\phi_n\rangle^{\mathrm{TR}}_h$.
\end{thm}

The proof is given after \cref{prop:mod-Rtr}; it needs two elementary lemmas. Its quoted inputs are \cite[Thm.~4.1]{DuninBarkowski:2012bw}, as recalled in \cite[\S2.6]{Dunin-Barkowski:2015caa}, with the compatibility condition \cite[Prop.~4.3 (1)]{Dunin-Barkowski:2015caa} and the sentence preceding \cite[Thm.~8.1]{Dunin-Barkowski:2015caa}, and the action of $R$-matrices of \cite{PPZ15} (inputs (Q) of \cref{sec:int-inputs}).
